# final-project
My project analyzes a dataset of labor certification petitions in order to better understand case outcomes and trends in employers and wages. The repository has three notebooks that analyze a Kaggle dataset (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/jboysen/us-perm-visas). 

What are labor certification petitions?
These are the applications necessary for an employer to sponsor someone for permanent residency. They include the prevailing wage and must demonstrate a need for employees.

Why is this project important?
Immigration law and legal processes have historically been kept convoluted and confusing to restrict access to information and make it more difficult for non-citizens to work in the United States and achieve citizenship. Through analyzing available data, we can provide insight into the process and hopefully promote transparency for employees and employers in the petition process. Specifically, the project seeks to identify trends in applicant backgrounds, wages offered, and the timeline of approved and denied processes. 

1. ['1_data_cleaning.ipynb'](code/1_data_cleaning.ipynb) loads the raw CSV and goes through the data, coercing numeric fields and creating a cleaned dataset. I assigned this dataset to "perm_visas_clean.parquet". This makes the data simpler and allows us to access it in the other notebooks
2. ['2_visualizations.ipynb'](code/2_visualizations.ipynb) takes in 'perm_visas_clean.parquet' and provides visualizations of case outcomes by year, the top occupations of the petitions, and the distribution of annual wages of the sought-after jobs.
3. ['3_wages_linear_regression.ipynb'](code/3_wages_linear_regression.ipynb) takes in 'perm_visas_clean.parquet' and creates an OLS regression estimating how employer size, wage level, required education, and year of filing relate to the offered wage.
4. ['4_processing_length_kernel_density_estimate.ipynb'](code/4_processing_length_kernel_density_estimate.ipynb) takes in 'perm_visas_clean.parquet' and examines whether processing time predicts denial. It uses processing_days as a predictor of success. 

The project reveals that the filing volume shifted across years and the petition volume is concentrated is a small number of occupations. The regression model reveals which factors impact offered wage the most. The whiskers indicate how precisely the effect is estimated. The plot illustrates that education level has a significant impact, especially Doctorate, Master's, and Bachelor's degrees. The highest impact is from other education experiences such as certifications or specializations that demonstrate the employee's expertise or skill is required and hard to find in the U.S. The visualization also shows a negative effect for holding only a high school degree. This visualization helps us understand the visa applications being received and their correlation to wages. The second linear regression suggests a relationship between more processing days and denied applications. This insight is practically helpful for employers and employees waiting on decisions and could suggest that denied applications are looked into further or put on the backburner throughout the process.
